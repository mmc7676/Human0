Solar system
Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag to pan. Click any dot for live specs. Solar system: teal dots are human-made; distances are true 3D in AU. Zoom out past the outer Oort Cloud (about 100,000 AU) and the view switches to the Local Bubble in light years; click the Sun there to come back. Local Bubble: every naked-eye star (V≤5.8) within 1,000 ly, moving along its measured space velocity when bubble time runs; the purple wireframe is the Zucker et al. 2022 spherical bubble model (radius 165 pc); teal names have planets. Zoom out past 15,000 ly, or press Galaxy, for the third scale: the Milky Way in thousands of light years, with the black hole Sgr A* and its S-stars, the dark matter halo, 161 globular clusters and 50 satellite galaxies on measured orbits, pulsars, supernova light shells and gravitational-wave fronts. Orange spheres are supernova light, blue sheets and spheres are gravitational waves, both moving at light speed; purple marks dark matter. Reverse time is counted in years BCE on the same 12-month calendar, with 0 CE as year zero (historians call that year 1 BCE).
Data: stars from HYG Database v4.1 (CC BY-SA 4.0, astronexus); planets from the Open Exoplanet Catalogue (MIT), snapshot 2026-11-12; bubble model from Zucker et al. 2022, Nature 601, 334; spacecraft from NASA JPL Horizons; asteroids from the JPL Small-Body Database. Galaxy scale: clusters and satellites from galpy named objects (Vasiliev & Baumgardt 2021; Pace et al. 2022); pulsars and historical supernovae from Stellarium data (GPL-2.0-or-later; ATNF Pulsar Catalogue v2.9.0); mass model from gala MilkyWayPotential v1 (Price-Whelan 2017; Bovy 2015); Sgr A* from GRAVITY Collaboration 2022; S-stars from Gillessen et al. 2017 and later papers and nearest black holes, both via Wikipedia tables (CC BY-SA 4.0), accessed 2026-11-15.
