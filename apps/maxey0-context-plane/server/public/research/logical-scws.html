Logical Structured Context Worlds

Exact context boundaries without a runtime, with user-side ChatGPT advertising privacy as a first application

Mike Cohan and Maxey0-Claude (Opus 5.5) · 30 September 2026 · 17 pages

Download PDFOpen full screen

Summary

Where the owner of a boundary does not control the runtime (inside another vendor's product, between tenants, or between agents sharing one context), the boundary cannot be a process or sandbox. The paper defines the logical SCW: a boundary that is a declarative policy object, enforced by one pure evaluator on every egress path, governed by fixed invariants no setting can weaken, and required to name the rule behind every withheld signal. It pairs this with Maxey0's five typed memory tiers (Working, Episodic, Semantic, Persistent, Procedural) and an evidence-gated promotion gate. The first application is a user-side advertising boundary for ChatGPT Free and Go users, tested on a real ChatGPT data export; a seeded property test of 20,000 random cases found zero violations of 19 per-case checks. It states plainly that it cannot bind OpenAI's ad system and labels everything beyond the user's own policy as advisory.

Questions and answers

What makes an SCW logical?
Its boundary is a declarative policy enforced by one pure evaluator on every egress path, not a process or sandbox.
What are the memory tiers?
Working, Episodic, Semantic, Persistent and Procedural, joined by an evidence-gated promotion gate.
What was tested?
A seeded property test of 20,000 random profiles, worlds, messages and histories: zero violations of 19 per-case checks.
What is the limit?
It cannot bind OpenAI's ad system; it gives the user an exact account of their own policy and labels the rest advisory.
